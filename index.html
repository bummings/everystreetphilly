I'm runnin every street in Philadelphia

Philadelphia contains something like 2,500 miles of streets, depending on who is counting, what qualifies as a street, and whether certain alleys, ramps, service roads, and suspicious asphalt appendages have successfully completed the necessary paperwork. Laid end to end, the whole system would reach most of the way across the country. Unfortunately, the streets have declined to be laid end to end. They remain arranged in Philadelphia.

The project is therefore to run them where they are: broad avenues, dead ends, industrial access roads, half-blocks, forgotten lanes, and streets that appear perfectly ordinary on a map but turn out to contain a fence, a staircase, a loading dock, or a man who would like to know what exactly is going on here. The official mileage is only the beginning. Getting through the network requires doubling back, crossing over, circling around, and occasionally discovering that the city has invented a new block since yesterday.
